Research thesis

My master’s thesis, part of the M.S. in Biomedical Engineering at Georgia Tech (expected August 2027), is completed in the Exoskeleton and Prosthetic Intelligent Controls Lab. My research focuses on exoskeleton design and on integrating task-agnostic and optimal control paradigms to reduce the energetic cost of human movement.

Work so far

  • Designed and fabricated carbon-fiber hip and ankle exoskeleton structures, including component geometry, mold design, and composite layup.
  • Built modular wearable hardware with orthotic interfaces, brackets, and connectors, with sliding adjustments, configurable straps, and quick-swap components to fit different users.
  • Designed experiments using motion capture and encoder-based measurements to quantify human-device interface coupling and force attenuation through wearable interfaces.
  • Optimized joint-state informed control strategies for hip and ankle exoskeletons.
