About me

I’m actively seeking full-time opportunities starting Fall 2027. I’m interested in the development and control of wearable robotic interfaces for rehabilitation and augmentation of human movement. Let’s connect!

I’m a 2nd-year master’s student in Biomedical Engineering at Georgia Institute of Technology, completing my thesis in the Exoskeleton and Prosthetic Intelligent Controls Lab. My research focuses on exoskeleton design and on integrating task-agnostic and optimal control paradigms to reduce the energetic cost of human movement.

Previously, I completed my B.S. in Mechanical Engineering with an additional major in Biomedical Engineering at Carnegie Mellon University. During that time I was involved in the CIA Buggy racing team, did research in metamaterials and bioinspired soft actuators, and shadowed at Union Orthotics and Prosthetics. I also interned at a startup working on design for manufacturing and small-scale manufacturing of high-end at-home Pilates equipment.

I grew up in Roswell, Georgia, moved to Palo Alto, California in 2019, and now live in San Francisco.

CIA Buggy Racing Team

During undergrad, I was co-president of the CIA Buggy Racing Team. Buggy is a century-old sport and tradition at Carnegie Mellon in which independent student teams design, build, and race carbon fiber gravity racers. CIA Buggy operates with 5 main subfunctions: Mechanics & Build, Pushers, Drivers, Data, and Social. As co-president of CIA Buggy, I was continuously working with our officers to create the best experience for our members and to optimize these team functions and have a successful raceday, which led to 1st place wins in 2024 and 2025.
